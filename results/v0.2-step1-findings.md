Load-once is a 5.6× win over v1 and 1.6× faster than vLLM's Triton AWQ kernel, but still slower than fp16.

## Diagnosis (qkv_proj, M=1)
1. **Unpack math is no longer the bottleneck**: dropping dequant (31–45 us) or even the interleave
   (load + dot only: 40–44 us) barely changes time.
2. **The main loop is latency-bound, not bandwidth-bound**: 12.6 MB in ~40 us = ~330 GB/s (7% of HBM).
   A plain 12.6 MB device copy takes 12.2 us. With 96 CTAs (< 132 SMs) each streaming
   its column strip serially, there are too few bytes in flight (Little's law).
3. **Measurement matters**: `do_bench` includes launch overhead (empty kernel ≈ 4.8 us) and
   split-K adds two extra launches (zero-init, fp32→fp16 cast). Under CUDA graphs (how vLLM / SGLang
   run decode):

| M=1, CUDA graph, us | fp16 | v2 split-K 1 | v2 best split-K |
|---|---|---|---|
| qkv_proj | 14.1 | 36.8 | **22.9** (sk4, BN 64) |
| down_proj | 29.7 | 80.9 | **39.1** (sk8, BN 64) |
